# Black-Scholes Call Option Theta and Charm

The Black-Scholes model provides formulas for calculating option Greeks, including theta and charm for call options. 
Theta measures the rate of change in an option's price with respect to time (time decay), given by the formula: $\Theta = -\frac{S e^{-r_f \tau} \sigma}{2\sqrt{\tau}} n(d_1) - r_d K e^{-r_d \tau} N(d_2) + r_f S e^{-r_f \tau} N(d_1)$. Charm, also known as delta decay, measures the instantaneous rate of change of delta with respect to time, calculated as: $$\text{Charm} = -e^{-r_f \tau} \left[ \frac{n(d_1)}{2\tau \sigma \sqrt{\tau}} \left(2r_f \tau - \frac{d_2 \sigma \sqrt{\tau}}{d_1}\right) + (r_f - r_d)N(d_1) \right]$$ In these formulas, S is the current stock price, τ is the time to expiration, σ is the volatility, r_f is the foreign interest rate (or dividend yield for stocks), r_d is the domestic interest rate (or risk-free rate for stocks), K is the strike price, N(x) is the cumulative normal distribution function, n(x) is the standard normal probability density function, and d1 and d2 are the standard Black-Scholes parameters. These calculations are crucial for options traders and risk managers to understand and manage time-related risks associated with options positions.
